## Durable cautions

### Preserve independent first views

Social influence can reduce answer diversity without improving group accuracy. Council therefore keeps first-round conclusions independent.

- Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer, and Helbing (2011). [How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1008636108).

### Give members distinct evidence responsibilities

Groups can repeat shared information and miss facts held by only one member. Council therefore makes evidence and boundaries explicit in each persona card.

- Stasser and Stewart (1992). [Discovery of Hidden Profiles by Decision-Making Groups](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.63.3.426).

### Use structured conflict conditionally

Devil's advocacy can improve some decisions. Dialectical inquiry is not consistently better across task types. Council therefore treats challenge as one pattern, not the default.

- Schwenk (1990). [Effects of devil's advocacy and dialectical inquiry on decision making](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/074959789090051A).

### Do not assume repeated debate is better

Multi-agent debate can help on some tasks. Broad comparisons do not show reliable gains over strong single-agent baselines. Council therefore adds another round only for a named purpose.

- Du et al. (2023). [Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325).
- Zhang et al. (2025). [If Multi-Agent Debate is the Answer, What is the Question?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08788).

## Transfer limits

- Human group research supplies design hypotheses. It does not prove that language-model agents behave the same way.
- Results depend on task shape, evidence quality, member capability, and synthesis quality.
- Delphi and Socratic methods come from human facilitation contexts. Treat them as task-specific patterns, not universal agent protocols.
- A council result is not ground truth. Primary evidence and direct tests remain stronger than agreement.
