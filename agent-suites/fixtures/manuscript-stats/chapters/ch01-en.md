# The Night Train

We boarded at Vienna a little after ten, carrying more bread than we could ever eat. The conductor checked our tickets twice and told us the dining car had closed at nine. Nobody minded. There was a window that opened halfway, and the air that came through it smelled of rain and diesel.

By midnight the corridor was quiet. A man in the next compartment was reading a paperback with the cover torn off, and he laughed at it every few pages. I never learned what it was. We crossed into Hungary while I slept, and I woke to a station sign I couldn't pronounce and a sky turning the colour of weak tea.
